Repiping & Whole-Home Pipe Replacement in Lancaster, CA

Call (714) 750-8637

Rusty Water, Repeated Leaks, or Pressure That Keeps Dropping?

If you've had more than one pipe leak in the past year or two, notice rusty water throughout the house rather than at just one fixture, or have pressure that's been quietly declining for a while, the underlying pipe material itself may be the actual problem - not any one specific leak. Repiping replaces the aging supply lines causing the pattern, rather than treating each new leak as its own isolated repair.

Why it happens here

Galvanized Pipe Is the Usual Reason in Lancaster

Homes built before the mid-1980s in Lancaster often still have original galvanized steel supply lines, which have a real, well-understood lifespan of about 40 to 50 years before internal corrosion narrows the pipe and eventually causes pinhole leaks. Lancaster's extremely hard water accelerates that corrosion further. Our water heater repair page and slab leak page both touch on this same material issue from different angles - repiping is the direct fix once a home's galvanized system has reached the point where individual repairs are no longer the more sensible option.

Material choice

Copper or PEX: What We Actually Install

Copper has a long track record and resists corrosion far better than galvanized steel, making it a durable, well-understood choice even in a hard-water market. PEX, a flexible plastic tubing, has become the more common choice in newer installations because it doesn't corrode at all, requires fewer joints, and generally costs less to install since it can be run more flexibly through existing wall cavities. Neither material eliminates mineral scale from hard water, but neither corrodes the way steel does - which is the specific failure mode a repipe is meant to solve.

What to expect

How the Job Actually Goes

  • 1

    Assessment. We confirm which lines need replacement - sometimes it's the whole house, sometimes specific at-risk sections.

  • 2

    Access. New lines are run following the original routing where possible to minimize how much drywall needs opening.

  • 3

    Water service. Interruptions are limited to the specific section being worked on at any given time, not the whole house for the entire job.

  • 4

    Permits and inspection. A repipe is permitted work, inspected by the local building department before it's signed off.

Full or partial

Does the Whole House Need It?

Not always. If only one section of a home's plumbing - say, a single bathroom's supply lines - is showing failure while the rest of the system tests fine, a partial repipe targeting just that section is a reasonable middle step. A full repipe makes more sense once multiple sections are aging in parallel, since it avoids doing the same disruptive work again in a few years on the next failing section.

Living through it

What a Repipe Is Actually Like Day to Day

Most households can still live in the home during a repipe, since water is only shut off to the specific section being worked on rather than the whole house at once. Some rooms will have limited access for a day at a time while walls are opened and closed, and a small amount of drywall touch-up is typically needed afterward - we coordinate that so it's a known part of the plan rather than a surprise at the end. For a business or a household that genuinely can't have any water interruption at all, we can also discuss scheduling the entire job in smaller phases built around your specific needs and daily routine.
