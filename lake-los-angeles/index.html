Lake Los Angeles Is a Large, Spread-Out High-Desert Community

Lake Los Angeles is an unincorporated Los Angeles County community roughly 17 miles east of Palmdale and about 20 miles from central Lancaster. The community takes its name from a natural lake that developers refilled in the late 1960s to sell lots, a lake that has since dried up, and today it's one of the larger population centers in the eastern Antelope Valley, with over 13,000 residents, large lots, scattered development, and the same hot, dry high-desert climate pattern found throughout the region.

Wells, Septic, and Large Parcels Change the Starting Point

A meaningful share of Lake Los Angeles properties sit on large unimproved or semi-improved lots outside the public sewer and water districts, which means private wells and septic systems come up far more often here than in Lancaster's core. That changes where we start troubleshooting a water pressure or quality complaint, and it's why septic tank pumping, covered on our septic tank pumping page, is a regular part of the work we do out this way rather than an edge case.

Before we arrive

If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak

Lake Los Angeles's large lots and mix of well-fed and septic properties mean a burst pipe can behave a little differently here. Here's what to do the moment you find one, before we arrive.

1

Shut Off the Main Valve

Stops new water from making the damage worse while help is on the way.

2

Kill Power to Wet Areas

Switch off the breaker for any room with standing water before touching anything electrical.

3

Clear the Immediate Area

Move people, pets, and valuables away from the leak until a technician has assessed it.

4

Call Our 24/7 Line

Tell the dispatcher it's an active leak so a technician is routed to you first.
