Rural by design

Antelope Acres Is Meant to Stay Rural

Antelope Acres is an unincorporated rural community about 13 miles northwest of Lancaster, centered near Avenue D (State Route 138) and 90th Street West, designated as a rural town center under Los Angeles County planning. Residents here have actively favored keeping the area's low-density, rural character rather than following Lancaster's more urbanized growth pattern. That planning designation shapes what infrastructure exists here, most notably, this is one of the areas we cover where septic systems are the norm rather than a connection to Lancaster's public sewer district.

Why this matters for plumbing

Our Septic Work Concentrates Right Here

Because Antelope Acres sits largely outside the public sewer district's boundary, our septic tank pumping and septic tank repair calls concentrate more heavily in this specific area than almost anywhere else we serve. If you're a homeowner here and haven't had your septic system's pumping history confirmed, that's a genuinely relevant first question given how common septic-dependent properties are in this community specifically.

Service Area

Large lots, real distances

What Rural Zoning Actually Means for Service

Antelope Acres' rural residential zoning keeps minimum lot sizes large, which means properties here are genuinely more spread out than a typical Lancaster subdivision, fewer households per mile, longer driveways, and sometimes a well as the water source alongside the septic system on the wastewater side. Both of those realities shape how we approach a service call here: confirming well versus municipal water upfront, and building in realistic travel time between more widely spaced properties.

A community that chose this

Rural by Resident Preference, Not Just Circumstance

Antelope Acres residents have specifically expressed a preference for keeping the area's rural, unincorporated character rather than following the path of denser development seen elsewhere in the Antelope Valley. That matters for how we think about the community: it's not an underdeveloped edge of Lancaster waiting to catch up, it's a place that's chosen its own low-density identity, and the septic-and-well infrastructure common here is a genuine, ongoing feature of the area rather than something expected to disappear.

What that means for water quality

Private Wells Add a Layer Municipal Customers Don't Deal With

A property on a private well is responsible for its own water quality in a way a municipal customer isn't, there's no utility treating the supply before it reaches your taps. That doesn't change the hard-water scale mechanics covered on our water softener page, since Antelope Acres draws from the same regional groundwater, but it does mean well-specific factors like sediment, iron, or bacterial content are worth testing for separately from the plumbing-side hardness issue.

Coordinating well and septic together

Two Systems, One Property, One Call

A property running both a private well and a septic system has two separate infrastructure pieces that need to be kept a safe distance apart and maintained on their own schedules, and problems with one can sometimes get mistaken for problems with the other. We ask about both when we're assessing an Antelope Acres property, since a full picture of the water-in, waste-out system on a self-sufficient rural property gives a more accurate diagnosis than looking at just the plumbing fixtures inside the house alone.

Before we arrive

If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak

Antelope Acres' well-and-septic properties and wide-open lots mean an active leak needs a slightly different first response than a municipal-connected home. Here's what to do before we arrive.

1

Shut Off the Main Valve

Stops new water from making the damage worse while help is on the way.

2

Kill Power to Wet Areas

Switch off the breaker for any room with standing water before touching anything electrical.

3

Clear the Immediate Area

Move people, pets, and valuables away from the leak until a technician has assessed it.

4

Call Our 24/7 Line

Tell the dispatcher it's an active leak so a technician is routed to you first.
