The most common call

Dripping Faucets Wear Out Faster in Lancaster

A dripping faucet almost always comes down to a worn valve cartridge or a degraded rubber seal or O-ring inside the faucet body. Lancaster's hard water accelerates that wear on both parts - mineral deposits interfere with a clean seal and gradually pit and score the surfaces a cartridge depends on to shut off cleanly, which is why a faucet here can start dripping years sooner than the same model would in a soft-water city.

When it can't wait

Emergency Faucet and Supply Valve Failures

A supply line or shutoff valve that cracks or lets go under a sink, behind a washing machine, or at an outdoor spigot can flood a room quickly, because the pipe is under full pressure all the time. Find the angle stop under the fixture and turn it clockwise. If it will not close, or it is the part that failed, shut off the main water valve instead. Mop up, run a fan, and call us.

Old multi-turn valves often seize or crumble when someone finally tries to close them, which is how a small drip becomes an emergency. We replace failed valves and braided supply lines so the next shutoff actually works.

What we actually fix

Beyond the Drip

  • ●

    Low flow from a specific faucet. Usually a clogged aerator screen collecting mineral scale, an easy and inexpensive fix.

  • ●

    A faucet that's loose or wobbles at the base. Often a loosened mounting nut under the sink, sometimes combined with a failing supply line connection.

  • ●

    Leaking under the sink. Can be the faucet's own supply connections, the P-trap, or the sink's drain assembly - each has a different fix, and we confirm which before assuming.

  • ●

    A sink that won't drain properly. Sometimes the faucet and sink area, sometimes actually a drain line issue covered on our drain cleaning page.

Repair or replace

When a New Faucet Makes More Sense

A single worn cartridge is usually a straightforward, inexpensive repair on most faucet brands. But an older faucet that's needed more than one repair, or one where replacement cartridges are hard to source because the model is discontinued, is often better replaced outright - especially once the finish itself shows pitting or corrosion from years of hard-water exposure that a new cartridge won't fix. We'll tell you honestly which situation you're in rather than defaulting to the more expensive option.

Sink installation

New Sink and Faucet Installation

Whether you're upgrading a kitchen sink during a remodel or replacing a failing bathroom sink, installation includes confirming the countertop cutout matches the new fixture, connecting supply lines and drain assembly correctly, and sealing the sink to the counter to prevent water intrusion underneath - a real concern in a home where a slow, unnoticed leak under a counter can go undetected for a long time.

We can also advise on faucet finishes and cartridge types that tend to hold up better under Lancaster's specific water conditions.

Material choice

Cartridge Types and Why Some Hold Up Better Here

Ceramic disc cartridges generally resist hard-water wear better than older rubber-washer designs, since the ceramic surfaces don't degrade the way rubber does when repeatedly exposed to mineral-heavy water. Not every faucet uses the same cartridge style, and not every brand's ceramic option performs identically, so when we're replacing a cartridge or recommending a new faucet, we factor in how that specific design has actually held up in Lancaster's water rather than going by a manufacturer's generic specifications alone.

Preventive care

Small Habits That Extend a Faucet's Life

Periodically removing and cleaning the aerator screen - even before flow noticeably drops - keeps mineral buildup from accumulating to the point where it restricts flow or damages the screen itself. Avoiding excessive force when shutting off a faucet handle also matters more here than in a soft-water area, since a cartridge already under stress from mineral wear is more vulnerable to damage from being forced closed too hard.

Kitchen vs. bathroom

They Wear Differently

A kitchen faucet sees far more daily use and heavier temperature swings than most bathroom faucets, which means its cartridge and seals typically wear out sooner even with the same water quality. A pull-down or pull-out kitchen sprayer hose is also its own wear item, with a separate failure pattern from the faucet body itself - worth mentioning specifically if that's the part actually giving you trouble rather than the faucet handle.

Showers and tubs

Shower Valves, Tub Spouts, and Showerheads

A shower valve that drips after it is turned off, a tub spout that leaks from the wall, or water that soaks the floor outside the tub all point to a worn cartridge, seat, or connection behind the wall. Many of these repairs need only a small access opening.

If water is staining a ceiling below the bathroom, shut off the water to the bathroom or the whole house and call, because moisture inside a wall keeps working after the dripping stops.

Outdoors and utility areas

Outdoor Spigots, Laundry Sinks, and Appliance Lines

Hose bibs that drip from the handle or the wall, laundry sink faucets that no longer seal, and the shutoff valves behind washing machines are among the most common small leaks that turn big. We also replace the valve and line feeding a refrigerator or ice maker when it leaks onto the kitchen floor, and we can add or replace outdoor faucets for a yard project.
