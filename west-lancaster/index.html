What West Lancaster Means Here

West Lancaster is a name locals and reference sources use for the residential side of the city west of downtown. Wikipedia's entry on Lancaster identifies West Lancaster as the home of the Prime Desert Woodland Preserve, and Wikipedia's list of Lancaster parks places the preserve at 43201 35th Street West and Rawley Duntley Park at 3334 West Avenue K. (city entry, parks list)

Homes.com's neighborhood guide describes the area with streets numbered from about 20th to 40th Street West, the Avenue J, K and L corridors, West Lancaster Boulevard, and a retail strip along State Route 14. (listing-site guide) Those are descriptions from a listing site and not official boundaries. We describe the area by those anchor streets and confirm coverage by address when you call. Farther west and north, the rural communities such as Antelope Acres have their own page.

Homes and lots

Single-Family Homes on Larger Lots

The same listing-site guide describes mostly single-family homes, a mix of midcentury houses and newer homes with Spanish Revival styling, and lots that are larger than typical. It does not give reliable building ages, so we do not assume them, and the home in front of us is what we work from.

Larger lots mean more outdoor plumbing, and that is general plumbing information and not a claim about any one street. Longer runs between the meter and the house, hose bibs, irrigation connections and yard lines are all places where a leak can run for a while before anyone sees it. Signs to watch for are a water meter that keeps moving when everything is off, a soft or unusually green patch of yard, or a bill that jumps with no change in use. Our leak detection page explains how we find the source without digging more than necessary.

Water heaters

Water Heaters, Garages and the Bracing Rule

Single-story homes with garages often put the water heater in the garage, though your home may differ. California Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped, so a replacement or a new installation should include proper strapping. See our pages on water heater repair and water heater installation for the signs that point toward repair or replacement.

If the tank is leaking from the body, close the cold water valve at the top and shut off power or the gas control, then call. A tank leak is usually a replacement and not a repair.

Water heater or yard leak? Describe the problem and we will explain what comes next.

Hard water

Hard Water Is a Valley-Wide Fact

The LA County Waterworks District 40 water quality report for the Antelope Valley lists total hardness between 30 and 320 ppm across its system. (report) That is a wide range, so we do not tell you what your tap is without testing it. Check your water bill to see who your provider is, and test your own water if hardness is a concern. Where hardness is high, scale tends to shorten water heater life and clog aerators and valves, and our water softener page covers what can be done about it.

Sewer or septic

Know What Your Property Is Connected To

Some properties in and around Lancaster are on a septic system and some are on a public sewer, and you should confirm which applies to yours. If you are sewered, the County Public Works page says the lateral is owned entirely by the property owner, for its whole length including under the street. (source) If you are on septic, regular pumping and a working tank matter, and our septic pumping page explains how that service works. Before any yard digging, call 811 through DigAlert.
