Littlerock Sits Where the Desert Meets the San Gabriels

Littlerock is an unincorporated Los Angeles County community roughly 16 miles southeast of Lancaster, at the base of the San Gabriel Mountains along Pearblossom Highway. It's known locally for its fruit stands and orchards, a genuine agricultural streak that sets it apart from the flatter high-desert tracts closer to Lancaster's core, and for Charlie Brown Farms, a well-known roadside stop that draws visitors from across the Antelope Valley. Its foothill elevation brings slightly cooler, wetter winters than the valley floor, with real snow some years where Lancaster proper sees none.

What's different here

Foothill Terrain Changes the Plumbing Picture

Littlerock's sloped, rockier foothill terrain means more properties sit on private wells and septic systems than in central Lancaster, and seasonal runoff from the San Gabriels during a heavy winter storm can affect drainage and buried pipe differently than it does on the flat desert floor. We factor in that terrain difference when troubleshooting a water pressure or drainage issue here rather than assuming Lancaster's typical flat-lot pattern applies, and septic tank pumping, covered on our septic tank pumping page, is a regular part of the work we do in Littlerock.

Before we arrive

If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak

Littlerock's foothill terrain and mix of well-fed and septic properties mean a burst pipe can behave a little differently here. Here's what to do the moment you find one, before we arrive.

1

Shut Off the Main Valve

Stops new water from making the damage worse while help is on the way.

2

Kill Power to Wet Areas

Switch off the breaker for any room with standing water before touching anything electrical.

3

Clear the Immediate Area

Move people, pets, and valuables away from the leak until a technician has assessed it.

4

Call Our 24/7 Line

Tell the dispatcher it's an active leak so a technician is routed to you first.
