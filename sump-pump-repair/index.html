Sump Pump Repair in Lancaster, CA

An honest starting point

A Genuine Minority of Lancaster Homes Have One

Sump pumps exist to move water out of a point that sits below the surrounding grade - a basement floor, a crawlspace low spot, or a below-grade utility room. Because the overwhelming majority of Antelope Valley homes are built on a concrete slab with no below-grade space at all, most Lancaster properties simply have nowhere for a sump pump to do its job. This page covers the pump itself: repair, replacement, and the handful of situations where one is actually in play here, the same honest minority of properties covered on our basement waterproofing page.

Where we actually find them

Basements, Ejector Pits, and Custom Foothill Builds

The properties where a sump pump actually turns up fall into a few recognizable groups: the small number of genuine basements scattered through older or custom-built Lancaster homes, below-grade utility or laundry rooms on a handful of raised-foundation builds, and ejector-pump setups serving a below-grade bathroom addition. We also see more of them on the custom hillside properties out toward Leona Valley, where lot grading sometimes creates a genuine low point that a slab-on-grade tract home in central Lancaster never would. If you're not sure which category your property falls into, describing the room layout on the phone usually settles it quickly.

What we actually repair

The Pump, the Switch, and the Discharge Line

  • ●

  • ●

    Float switch failure - the single most common point of failure, and often mistaken for a dead pump.

  • ●

    Check valve failure letting discharged water flow back into the pit between cycles.

  • ●

  • ●

    Discharge line clogs or, on a handful of cold winter nights, freezing at an exposed exterior run.

  • ●

    Full pump replacement and ejector pump service for below-grade bathroom and laundry setups.

A common mix-up

Sump Pump vs. Ejector Pump vs. HVAC Condensate Pump

These three get confused constantly, and they are not the same job. A sump pump moves groundwater or seepage out of a pit. An ejector pump moves actual wastewater from a below-grade bathroom or laundry room up to the sewer line, a heavier-duty job with different plumbing-code requirements. An HVAC condensate pump is a much smaller unit that clears water from an air handler and typically falls to an HVAC technician rather than a plumber. Tell us what the pump is actually connected to and we'll tell you honestly whether it's ours to fix.
